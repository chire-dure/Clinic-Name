What Are Dental Braces & How Do They Work?

Dental braces are precision orthodontic appliances designed to gently guide misaligned teeth, straighten dental arches, and correct functional bite discrepancies. By delivering continuous, calibrated physiological forces, braces signal the surrounding bone tissue to remodel naturally around the tooth roots.

3 Orthodontic Bracket Options Available

Traditional Metal Braces

Most Popular

Constructed from high-grade biocompatible stainless steel. Highly durable and reliable for treating all types of severe crowding and bite misalignment.

Customization: Change colorful elastic ligatures every monthly visit!

Best for: Teenagers, students, and budget-conscious patients.

Aesthetic Ceramic Braces

Discreet & Aesthetic

Made from translucent monocrystalline or tooth-colored ceramic brackets that blend subtly with your natural enamel.

Customization: Clear or pearl ligature bands for an invisible finish.

Best for: Working professionals, executives, and public-facing individuals.

Self-Ligating Braces

Low Friction

Features a built-in specialized sliding clip door mechanism holding the archwire, eliminating the need for elastic rubber bands and minimizing frictional resistance.

Comfort: Easier hygiene maintenance and gentler biological forces.

Best for: Patients wanting fewer emergency visits and faster adjustment sessions.

Who Needs Orthodontic Treatment?

Orthodontic treatment isn't just about cosmetic alignment—it's vital for long-term masticatory health, preventing premature tooth wear, and reducing plaque traps that lead to periodontal disease.

Severe Crowding & Overlap

Insufficient jaw space forcing teeth to twist or push in front of one another.

Tooth Gaps (Diastema)

Noticeable spaces between central incisors or premolars trapping food debris.

Overbite / Deep Bite

Upper front teeth excessively cover lower teeth, sometimes biting into lower gums.

Underbite & Crossbite

Lower teeth protruding past upper teeth, placing abnormal stress on jaw joints (TMJ).

Benefits & Important Considerations

Long-Term Benefits

  • Balanced Facial Symmetry: Straight teeth support natural lip posture and facial aesthetics.
  • Effortless Oral Hygiene: Removes tight food traps, reducing risk of tartar buildup and cavities.
  • Even Chewing Pressure: Protects enamel from premature fractures and relieves TMJ tension.
  • Lifetime Smile Confidence: A confident smile opens doors in career, social, and academic life.

Good Things to Know

  • Duration: Most full alignment cases span between 12 to 24 months.
  • Food Habits: Hard nuts, whole apples, and chewing ice must be avoided.
  • Monthly Visits: Regular 4–6 week adjustment visits keep your progress on schedule.
  • Retainer Phase: Clear or Hawley retainers protect your aligned smile after debonding.

Step-by-Step Orthodontic Journey

1

Diagnostic Consultation & Records

Complete clinical exam, panoramic x-rays, study cast impressions, and facial photos to map out your individualized biomechanical plan.

2

Bonding Day (Bracket Placement)

Teeth surfaces are meticulously cleaned and conditioned. Brackets are bonded with dental adhesive, and flexible nickel-titanium archwires are engaged.

3

Monthly Archwire Adjustments

Every 4 to 6 weeks, visit our clinic to switch ligature colors, activate teeth movements, and check overall oral hygiene.

4

Debonding & Retainer Delivery

Brackets are comfortably removed, adhesive is smoothly polished away, and your custom retainers are fitted to lock in your lifelong smile.

Braces Care & Eating Guide

Protect your brackets from snapping off and safeguard your enamel against white spots during active treatment:

Recommended Braces-Friendly Foods

  • • Steamed fish, soft chicken, and eggs
  • • Rice porridge (lugaw), oatmeal, pasta, and mashed potatoes
  • • Yogurt, smoothies, bananas, and soft cheeses
  • • Soups and tender cooked vegetables

Foods to Avoid

  • • Sticky candies: toffees, caramels, bubblegum
  • • Hard snacks: corn chips, popcorn kernels, ice cubes
  • • Biting into whole apples or corn on the cob (cut into small slices first!)
  • • Sugary sodas that pool around bracket bases

Orthodontic Relief Wax: We provide complimentary medical wax kits on your bonding day. If a bracket edge irritates your cheek during the first week, roll a pea-sized wax ball and apply it directly onto the bracket for instant comfort.
