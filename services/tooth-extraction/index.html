What is Tooth Extraction?

Tooth extraction is the clinical removal of a tooth from its socket in the alveolar jawbone. While our primary dental philosophy is always to conserve natural teeth whenever feasible, an extraction becomes necessary when a tooth has suffered structural damage or bacterial infection beyond biological repair.

We distinguish between two principal categories of dental extraction:

Simple Extraction

Performed on teeth visible above the gumline that can be gently luxated and lifted with dental forceps after administering localized numbing anesthesia.

Surgical Extraction (Odontectomy)

Required for teeth that have broken beneath the gumline or impacted third molars (wisdom teeth) trapped under bone, requiring delicate incision and sectional removal.

Our experienced dentists prioritize patient comfort at every stage, using premium local anesthetics so you experience pressure during the procedure, but no sharp discomfort.

When is a Tooth Extraction Recommended?

Extractions are recommended when leaving the affected tooth risks your systemic health, adjacent teeth, or bone integrity:

Severe Non-Restorable Decay: Deep cavities that have destroyed the crown and root structure beyond saving.

Impacted Wisdom Teeth: Third molars growing horizontally or trapped under gums, causing pericoronitis and swelling.

Severe Periodontal Bone Loss: Advanced gum disease that has destabilized the tooth root socket.

Orthodontic Crowding: Strategically clearing space when prescribed for comprehensive braces treatment.

Clinical Assessment Note: A digital periapical or panoramic dental x-ray must be evaluated before extraction to map the tooth root anatomy, bone condition, and proximity to the mandibular nerve or sinus cavities.

Benefits & Recovery Considerations

Clinical Benefits

  • Immediate Pain Relief: Halts agonizing toothaches caused by infected or necrotic pulp nerves.
  • Infection Containment: Prevents spreading abscesses and facial cellulitis from threatening nearby tissues.
  • Adjacent Tooth Protection: Stops impacted third molars from resorbing roots of healthy second molars.
  • Prepares for Implants: Clean removal creates a healthy foundation for future dental implant placement.

Post-Op Considerations

  • Blood Clot Protection: The initial blood clot must remain undisturbed to prevent painful "dry socket" (alveolar osteitis).
  • No Suction or Straws: Strictly avoid drinking through straws, smoking, or spitting forcefully for 72 hours.
  • Temporary Swelling: Mild jaw soreness and localized swelling are normal after surgical wisdom tooth procedures.
  • Replacement Planning: Non-wisdom teeth should ideally be replaced with an implant or bridge to prevent bite shifting.

What Happens at Your Appointment?

1

Digital Radiography & Exam

The dentist captures a sharp dental x-ray to determine root curvature, surrounding bone density, and proximity to adjacent structures.

2

Targeted Local Anesthesia

We administer a gentle topical numbing gel followed by local anesthetic. We test the area thoroughly before touching the tooth to ensure complete numbness.

3

Careful Elevation & Removal

Using specialized micro-instruments, the tooth is gently loosened within the periodontal ligament and extracted. For surgical cases, dissolvable sutures may be placed.

4

Hemostasis & Post-Op Briefing

A clean sterile gauze pack is positioned over the socket. We provide printed recovery instructions, prescribe medications if indicated, and schedule a follow-up.

Aftercare & Dry Socket Prevention

Your body begins healing the extraction site immediately by forming a natural protective blood clot. Follow these guidelines closely:

First 24 Hours

Bite firmly on the gauze pack for 45 to 60 minutes. Apply an ice pack on your cheek (15 mins on, 15 mins off) to reduce swelling. Eat cool, soft foods (ice cream, soup, lugaw, yogurt).

After 24 Hours

Gently rinse with warm salt water (1/2 teaspoon salt in a glass of warm water) 3 times a day after meals. Do not spit forcefully; let water gently roll out of your mouth into the sink.

Warning Signs to Report: If you develop severe throbbing pain radiating toward your ear after 3 days (symptom of dry socket), continuous heavy bleeding that does not slow with gauze pressure, or fever exceeding 38°C, contact our clinic immediately.
