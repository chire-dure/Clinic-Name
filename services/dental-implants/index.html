What are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is an artificial tooth root surgically placed directly into the alveolar jawbone to support a replacement tooth crown, bridge, or stabilized denture. Made from medical-grade biocompatible titanium, the implant undergoes a natural biological bonding process called osseointegration, during which living bone cells lock securely into the microscopic pores of the titanium post.

Traditional removable dentures and bridges only replace the visible portion of teeth above the gumline. Without a root stimulating the jawbone underneath, the surrounding bone gradually shrinks (resorption), causing neighboring teeth to shift and facial contours to appear prematurely aged.

Dental implants provide true root replacement, delivering chewing power indistinguishable from healthy natural teeth and protecting long-term alveolar bone volume.

Are You a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Implant treatment requires healthy systemic conditions and adequate physical foundation. Ideal candidates include:

Missing One or More Teeth: Seeking a permanent solution that does not require grinding down neighboring healthy teeth.

Adequate Bone Volume: Sufficient jawbone height and width to firmly anchor the titanium fixture, or readiness for bone grafting.

Healthy Oral Mucosa: Free from active periodontal disease, chronic gingivitis, or untreated oral infections.

Stable General Health: Non-smokers or controlled systemic health (e.g., well-managed blood pressure and diabetes).

Clinical Suitability Assessment: Implant success depends on comprehensive pre-surgical evaluation, including digital 3D CBCT scans. No dentist can guarantee 100% osseointegration, but clinical success rates internationally exceed 95% when rigorous protocols are followed.

Benefits & Considerations

Key Benefits

  • Lifelike Function & Appearance: Feels and chews just like a natural tooth, restoring full dietary enjoyment.
  • Preserves Neighboring Teeth: Unlike conventional bridges, adjacent teeth remain untouched with no enamel reduction.
  • Prevents Jawbone Shrinkage: Transmits chewing forces into the bone, stimulating bone density and facial fullness.
  • Long-Term Durability: With proper hygiene, implant fixtures can last for decades or a lifetime.

Considerations

  • Healing Timeline: Osseointegration requires an undisturbed healing period of 3 to 6 months before loading the permanent crown.
  • Surgical Procedure: Requires minor oral surgery under local anesthesia with slight post-op soreness and swelling.
  • Higher Initial Investment: Higher upfront cost than acrylic dentures, balanced by unmatched durability.
  • Smoking Risks: Tobacco use significantly increases risks of peri-implantitis and bone integration failure.

The 4 Treatment Stages

1

3D Radiographic Planning & Assessment

The dentist performs a clinical exam and analyzes 3D scans to measure bone density, nerve pathways, and select the optimal implant dimension.

2

Implant Fixture Placement

Under local anesthesia, a minor gum incision is made, and the titanium post is inserted into the bone. A healing cap or temporary tooth protects the site.

3

Osseointegration (Healing Period)

Over 3 to 6 months, natural bone remodeling integrates with the titanium surface, forming a rigid, permanent biological foundation.

4

Abutment & Custom Crown Delivery

An abutment connector is attached to the implant, and a custom-shaded ceramic or zirconia crown is securely fitted to complete your smile.

Implant Care & Lifelong Maintenance

While titanium cannot get cavities, the surrounding gum tissue can suffer from peri-implantitis if bacteria accumulate. Protect your investment with these practices:

Daily Interdental Cleaning

Floss daily around the implant abutment using implant-specific floss or a water flosser to dislodge biofilm from the gum cuff.

Routine 6-Month Recalls

Visit our clinic at [Your Address] every six months for specialized maintenance, where we verify screw torque, bone levels, and perform ultrasonic hygiene.

Medical Precaution: If you experience persistent mobility of the implant post, sudden bleeding around the gumline, or severe throbbing pain weeks after surgery, contact our clinic immediately for examination.
